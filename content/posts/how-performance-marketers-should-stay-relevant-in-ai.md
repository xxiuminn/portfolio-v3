Effectiveness vs efficiency. Which matters more?

Two weeks ago I was sitting in the crowd at Google Marketing Live, listening to Les Binet, a consultant, author, and probably the most cited name in marketing effectiveness research.

His argument: effectiveness (scale) beats efficiency (ROI).

Here's what I took away.

## Profit = ROI × Budget. Budget wins.

90% of profits come from budget and only 10% from ROI. How much you invest is 9x more important than how efficiently you spend it.

Marketers got this backwards. We became obsessed with ROAS, with squeezing more out of less.

## The ROI trap

When you optimise for ROI, you start making small decisions. Narrower targeting, sub-segment focus and cutting channels to protect your efficiency numbers.

Short-term ROI goes up. Scale, profit, and long-term brand growth go down.

As he said: "small thinking leads to small profits." Brands that think small eventually become small.

## What to do instead

**1. Set budget scientifically.** Keep your share of voice (SOV) above your share of market (SOM). Higher SOV drives higher sales. If you're punching below your weight in media spend, you're losing ground even when the numbers look fine.

**2. Watch your A/S ratio.** The higher your ad-to-sales ratio, the faster you grow. Budgets need to rise ahead of sales. If you only spend when sales are good, growth stalls the moment they aren't.

**3. Maximise profit, not ROI.** Use econometric modelling to understand the effect of ads on sales. War-game your budget scenarios to maximise net profit, not ROI.

**4. 60/40.** 60% brand building (broad reach, emotional, memorable) and 40% sales activation (tight targeting, informative, direct response).
